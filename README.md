# Abstract
Ischemic hepatitis (IH) is caused by numerous underlying conditions of shock when oxygenated blood is not adequately supplied to the liver, leading to hepatocyte death. The diagnosis is based on clinical history for the most part, as there are no specific diagnostic tests for this condition and no easy way to determine the outcome of IH. This novel study aims to use machine learning methods on the readings of biomarkers of real life patients suffering from IH to achieve a strong predictive performance for the outcome of IH. We employ real-patient data where various biomedical indicators, such as creatinine, international normalized ratio (INR), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine transaminase (ALT), and bilirubin are measured for patients suffering from IH. The data is collected from multiple centers in the United States by the  Acute Liver Failure Study Group (ALFSG), a multi-center network enrolling patients with this rare condition. The goal is to  use classification models like logistic regression and regression tree method, including Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (BART) and neural networks to predict patients' outcomes with IH accurately. Synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) and boosting techniques are applied to improve the prediction accuracy. We aim to identify the biomarkers that most significantly impact the accuracy of IH predictions. In our analysis, INR is selected consistently by all the models and bilirubin is noticeably significant in the models applied on the data without SMOTE.
